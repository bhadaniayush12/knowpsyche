You click the moment the screen turns green, but the number that comes back is slower than you expected. This placeholder paragraph tests the drop cap and the lead size.

## What a reaction time measures

A reaction time is the gap between something changing and you responding to it. In our [reaction time test](/reaction-time-test/) that means the screen changing colour and your click or tap.

Most of that gap is not your hand. It is the time it takes to notice the change and decide to move.

> **Key idea** Your **reaction time** is mostly noticing and deciding. The movement itself is the short part at the end.

## Why your number changes

The same person can get quite different numbers on different days. A few common reasons:

- **Tiredness.** A tired mind notices changes a little later.
- **Your device.** Screens and mice add delay you can't see.
- **Practice.** The first few tries are often the slowest.

> **Side note** Browser tests include your screen's and mouse's delay, so the numbers aren't directly comparable with lab studies.

### A quick look at devices

| Device part | What it adds | Can you change it? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Screen refresh | A few milliseconds | Sometimes, in settings |
| Mouse or trackpad | A few milliseconds | Try a wired mouse |
| Touch screen | Often a little more | Not really |

> One slow try tells you about that moment, not about you.

## Three things that help

1. **Sit comfortably** with your finger ready before you start.
2. **Look at the centre** of the box, not at the edges.
3. **Take the average**, not your best single try.
