# Psychology myths (KnowPsyche)

Background and future Learn articles only. Plain English. No statistics on purpose.
Check each point against the source before it goes on a page.

---

## 1. "Memory works like a video recorder"
- **The belief:** the brain records everything and you can replay it exactly.
- **What research shows:** remembering is rebuilding. Each time, the mind puts a memory back together from pieces, so details can shift or be added.
- **Why it matters for readers:** feeling sure about a memory does not mean it is exact.
- **Sources to check:** Loftus, E. F. (various, on the misinformation effect); Lilienfeld et al. (2010), *50 Great Myths of Popular Psychology.*

## 2. "Some people have a photographic memory"
- **The belief:** certain people take mental snapshots and recall them perfectly.
- **What research shows:** perfect recall of this kind has not been reliably shown in adults. People with astonishing memories typically use techniques (like picturing a familiar route and placing items along it) and practise a lot.
- **Try this:** group things into chunks and link them to pictures you know.
- **Sources to check:** Ericsson & Chase (1982); Maguire et al. (2003), on memory champions and the method of loci.

## 3. "We only use 10% of our brains"
- **The belief:** most of the brain sits unused.
- **What research shows:** there is no silent 90%. Even small areas of damage can cause real problems, and many parts of the brain work all the time.
- **Why it matters:** be careful of anyone selling a program to "unlock" the rest.
- **Sources to check:** Beyerstein (1999), *Whence cometh the myth that we only use 10% of our brains?*; Lilienfeld et al. (2010).

## 4. "You are either left-brained or right-brained"
- **The belief:** logical people use the left side, creative people the right.
- **What research shows:** the two sides do work differently on some tasks, but nearly everything you do uses both sides together.
- **Why it matters:** calling yourself "just logical" or "just creative" is a label, not a fact about your brain.
- **Sources to check:** Lilienfeld et al. (2010).

## 5. "Sugar makes children hyperactive"
- **The belief:** sweets cause wild behaviour.
- **What research shows:** controlled studies have not found that sugar changes children's behaviour. Parties are exciting, and adults expect sugar to cause a rush, so they notice the noise.
- **Why it matters:** expecting something can change what we notice.
- **Sources to check:** Wolraich, Wilson & White (1995), meta-analysis in *JAMA*; Hoover & Milich (1994) on parental expectation.

## 6. "If someone is very sure, their memory must be right"
- **The belief:** confidence equals accuracy.
- **What research shows:** people can be very confident and wrong. Being asked again and again, or hearing others agree, can raise confidence without making the memory more accurate.
- **Why it matters:** ask "what else do we know?", not just "how sure are you?"
- **Sources to check:** Wells & Olson (2003) on eyewitness confidence; Loftus (various).

## 7. "People with amnesia forget who they are"
- **The belief:** film-style amnesia where someone wakes up not knowing their name.
- **What research shows:** in real life, the more common problem is trouble forming new memories, while older memories and identity are often intact.
- **Sources to check:** Lilienfeld et al. (2010).

## 8. "Intelligence tests tell you how smart you are"
- **The belief:** one quick test gives a fixed number for your mind.
- **What research shows:** any single test is a snapshot and is affected by practice, mood, tiredness and how familiar the task is.
- **Why it matters for KnowPsyche:** this is why every result page says our bands are our own guide, not a diagnosis and not a ranking.

## 9. "Personality tests put you in a box"
- **The belief:** a type describes you for life.
- **What research shows:** people often change a little across their lives and situations. Types are a handy way to talk about patterns, not a scientific fact about who you are. The Enneagram in particular is used mostly for reflection, and researchers have not shown that its types are fixed categories.
- **Why it matters for KnowPsyche:** use the result as a conversation starter about yourself, not as a label.
- **Sources to check:** the Enneagram section of docs/content/part-1.md.

## 10. "Emotional intelligence is a fixed trait"
- **The belief:** you either have it or you don't.
- **What research shows:** the skills described under this name (noticing feelings, naming them, handling them) can be practised. Scientists also disagree on how best to define and measure it.
- **Why it matters for KnowPsyche:** this is why we describe low scores as "still developing".
- **Sources to check:** the Emotional Intelligence section of docs/content/part-2.md.
