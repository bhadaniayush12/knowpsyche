What is a good reaction time?

In browser tests like this one, most people land somewhere around 200–300 ms. Your device matters: screens, mice and touchscreens can each add tens of milliseconds before your brain is even involved.

What happens in those 250 ms

01 · See

Light hits your retina and the signal travels to the visual cortex at the back of your head.

02 · Decide

Your brain recognises the change and decides it is the signal you were waiting for.

03 · Act

A command runs down your spinal cord and nerves to the muscles in your finger.

What changes your score

  • Sleep and alertness — being tired is one of the biggest slow-downs.
  • Practice — you get a little faster as the task becomes familiar.
  • Age — reaction time tends to slow gradually as we get older.
  • Your device — monitor refresh rate and input lag add delay you cannot see.

Questions

Can I improve my reaction time?+

A little. Good sleep, being alert and practising the task help. Big jumps usually mean a faster device, not a faster brain.

Why is my score slower on a phone?+

Touchscreens and phone displays often add more delay than a desktop mouse and monitor.

Is this a medical test?+

No. It is an educational activity for curiosity and self-reflection, not a diagnosis.
