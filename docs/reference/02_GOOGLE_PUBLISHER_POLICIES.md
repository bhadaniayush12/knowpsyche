# Google Publisher Policies

## Scope

These are the publisher policies contained in the supplied source material.

Google states that publishers using Google ad code are required to comply with these policies. Non-compliance may result in ads being blocked, or an account being suspended or terminated.

The supplied source organizes policies into:

1. Content policies
2. Behavioral policies
3. Privacy-related policies
4. Requirements and other standards

---

# 1. Content Policies

## Illegal content

Content is not allowed when it:

- Is illegal
- Promotes illegal activity
- Infringes the legal rights of others

## Intellectual property abuse

Content is not allowed when it:

- Infringes copyright
- Sells or promotes counterfeit products

The supplied material references Google's handling of copyright complaints and DMCA notices.

## Dangerous or derogatory content

Content is not allowed when it:

- Incites hatred against, promotes discrimination of, or disparages an individual or group on protected/systemically marginalized characteristics described by Google
- Harasses, intimidates, or bullies individuals or groups
- Threatens or advocates physical or mental harm to oneself or others
- Promotes, glorifies, or condones violence or terrorism-related harmful activity
- Exploits others through extortion

Examples in the supplied material include hate-group promotion, harassment, self-harm advocacy, violent threats, terrorist content, and extortion-related material.

## Animal cruelty

Content is not allowed when it:

- Promotes cruelty or gratuitous violence towards animals
- Promotes sale of products obtained from endangered or threatened species

## Misrepresentative content — misleading representation

Content is not allowed when it:

- Misrepresents, misstates, or conceals information about the publisher
- Misrepresents the content creator or purpose of the content
- Misrepresents the content itself
- Falsely implies affiliation with or endorsement by another individual, organization, product, or service

Examples include impersonating Google products or misusing company logos.

## Misrepresentative content — unreliable and harmful claims

The supplied material prohibits:

- Demonstrably false claims that could significantly undermine trust in an electoral or democratic process
- Harmful health claims, or claims about a current major health crisis that contradict authoritative scientific consensus
- Content that contradicts authoritative scientific consensus on climate change

## Misrepresentative content — deceptive practices

The supplied material prohibits:

- Enticing users to engage with content under false or unclear pretenses
- Attempting to steal personal information or trick users into providing personal information
- Promoting products, services, or content with false, dishonest, or deceptive claims
- Coordinating with other sites/accounts while concealing or misrepresenting identity in covered political/social/public-interest contexts
- Misrepresenting or concealing country of origin or other material details in covered political/social/public-interest contexts when directing such content to users in another country

Example: phishing / social engineering.

## Manipulated media

Content is not allowed when it deceives users through manipulated media related to:

- Politics
- Social issues
- Matters of public concern

## Enabling dishonest behavior

Content is not allowed when it:

- Helps users mislead others
- Enables creation or distribution of fake/false documents
- Promotes hacking or cracking or unauthorized access to devices, software, servers, or websites
- Enables circumvention of copyright protection
- Enables illegal descrambling or unauthorized access
- Enables unauthorized surveillance or tracking of other people

The supplied material distinguishes authorized/private investigation and parental monitoring of underage children from unauthorized surveillance examples.

## Sexually explicit content

The supplied material does not allow:

- Graphic sexual text, images, audio, video, or games
- Non-consensual sexual themes
- Promotion of creation/distribution of sexually explicit or nude generated/altered content

## Compensated sexual acts

Content may not promote or facilitate sexual acts in exchange for compensation.

The supplied examples include prostitution, escort services, intimate massage, compensated dating, and similar arrangements.

## Mail order brides

Content may not facilitate marriage to a foreigner in the forms described by the supplied policy.

Examples include mail-order brides, international marriage brokers, and romance tours.

## Adult themes in family content

Content may not be presented as appropriate for a family audience while containing adult themes involving sex, violence, or unsuitable depictions involving children or popular children's characters.

## Child sexual abuse and exploitation

The supplied policy prohibits:

- Sexual exploitation or abuse of children
- Content promoting such exploitation
- Child grooming
- Sextortion involving children
- Sexualization of minors
- Child trafficking for commercial sexual exploitation

Google may take enforcement action including account disabling and reporting where applicable.

# 2. Behavioral Policies

## Dishonest declarations

Information provided by publishers for interaction with Google advertising systems must:

- Be materially accurate and complete
- Avoid misleading omissions
- Not be expressed deceptively or misleadingly

The supplied examples include inaccurate publisher information, inaccurate `ads.txt` / `app-ads.txt`, incorrect URLs/App IDs, and incorrect geolocation.

## Google-served ads interfering with content or user interactions

Google-served ads must not:

- Overlay or sit adjacent to navigation/action controls in a way that may cause unintended interactions
- Severely interfere with content consumption
- Push content off the display
- Appear on a dead-end screen where a user cannot exit without clicking the ad

## Ads on screens without publisher content / low-value content

Google-served ads are not allowed on screens:

- Without publisher content or with low-value content
- That are under construction
- Used for alerts, navigation, or other behavioral purposes

## Out-of-context ads

Google-served ads are not allowed:

- In background-running apps/pages
- Outside the display
- When user attention is expected elsewhere rather than on the ad's hosting screen

The association between the ad and publisher content must be clear.

## Replicated content

Google-served ads are not allowed on screens containing:

- Embedded/copied content from others without additional commentary, curation, or other added value

The supplied material also requires compliance with the intellectual-property policy.

## More ads or paid promotional material than publisher content

Google-served ads are not allowed on screens with:

- More ads or other paid promotional material than publisher content

## Unsupported languages

Content is not allowed when it is not primarily in one of Google's supported languages.

# 3. Privacy-Related Policies

## Personalized advertising

The supplied material restricts the use of Google platform products or Google ad code to select/target personalized ads or collect/use audience data based on:

- Activity by users known to be under 13
- Activity on child-directed sites/areas for users under 13
- Activity on adult, gambling, or government agency sites/apps
- Sensitive information such as health/medical history, detailed negative financial status, racial/ethnic origins, religious beliefs, criminal activity/allegations, political affiliation, trade union membership, and sexual behavior/orientation

The supplied material also includes U.S./Canada audience-targeting restrictions for housing, employment, and certain credit/banking/financial offers.

When using personalized advertising, the supplied material states that publishers must:

- Have the necessary rights to use audience data such as cookie lists
- Use required interest-based advertising notices where applicable
- Comply with applicable industry guidelines

## Privacy disclosures

Publishers must:

- Have and abide by a privacy policy
- Clearly disclose data collection, sharing, and usage caused by Google products/services
- Disclose technologies such as cookies, web beacons, IP addresses, or other identifiers where applicable
- Disclose that third parties may place/read cookies or use web beacons/IP addresses as a result of ad serving

The supplied material says publishers may use a prominent link to Google's "How Google uses data when you use our partners' sites or apps" page to help meet the disclosure obligation.

## Cookies on Google domains

Publishers must not:

- Set a cookie on Google's domains
- Modify, intercept, or delete cookies set on Google's domains

## Identifying users

Publishers must not:

- Pass information to Google that Google could use or recognize as personally identifiable information
- Use Google services to identify users or merge personally identifiable information with previously collected non-personally identifiable information without the required robust notice and prior affirmative opt-in consent
- Attempt to disaggregate Google's aggregate reporting

Publishers must also comply with the EU user consent policy where applicable.

## Device and location data

If precise geographic location data is collected, processed, or disclosed, the supplied policy requires:

- Disclosure through an interstitial or just-in-time notice of purposes, including applicable ad personalization, analytics, attribution, and partner sharing
- Express opt-in consent before collection, processing, or disclosure
- Encrypted transmission to Google
- Disclosure in applicable privacy policies

## COPPA

If a site/app/section is covered by COPPA and uses Google advertising services, the supplied material requires the appropriate child-directed treatment and prohibits interest-based advertising targeting based on covered under-13 activity.

# 4. Requirements and Other Standards

## Spam policies for Google web search

Google-served ads must not be placed on screens that violate Google's Spam policies for Google web search.

## Abusive experiences

Google-served ads must not be placed on screens containing abusive experiences.

## Malware or unwanted software

Google-served ads must not be placed on screens containing malware or software that may harm a computer, device, or network or gain unauthorized access.

The supplied examples include viruses, ransomware, worms, trojan horses, rootkits, keyloggers, dialers, spyware, rogue security software, and similar malicious programs.

Google-served ads must also not be placed on screens violating Google's Unwanted Software policy.

The supplied examples include:

- Not being transparent about software functionality
- Failing to explain installation implications
- Bundling software without user knowledge
- Making system changes without consent
- Making uninstall/disabling difficult
- Improper use of publicly available Google APIs when interacting with Google products/services

## Better Ads Standards

Google-served ads must not be placed on screens that do not conform to the Better Ads Standards.

## Authorized inventory

Google-served ads must not be placed on a domain using `ads.txt` when the publisher is not included as an authorized seller in that file.

## Sanctions compliance

The supplied source states that Google publisher products are unavailable to publishers in certain sanctioned countries/regions and cannot be used for or on behalf of restricted parties.

The source explicitly lists:

- Crimea
- Cuba
- So-called Donetsk People's Republic (DNR) and Luhansk People's Republic (LNR)
- Iran
- North Korea

The source also requires compliance with applicable sanctions and export-control regulations.

## Audit priority for a normal content website

For a standard public website seeking initial AdSense approval, an agent should pay particular attention to:

1. Original, valuable content
2. Scraped/copied/copyright-infringing content
3. Misrepresentation and deceptive claims
4. Hacking/unauthorized access or surveillance-related content
5. Restricted/prohibited content
6. Low-value or under-construction pages
7. Replicated third-party content without added value
8. Privacy policy and data disclosures
9. Spam-policy violations
10. Malware/unwanted software
11. Accurate publisher/site information

This priority is an audit strategy derived from the supplied policy set; it is not a claim that Google ranks these categories in this exact order.
